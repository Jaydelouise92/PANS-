## 2026-07-21 - Form Control Label Wrapping and Accessibility Links
**Learning:** HTML nested label wrapping (e.g. `<label><span>Label</span><input /></label>`) can sometimes cause accessibility and screen-reader mapping problems, especially when validation errors are also placed inside or outside the label without explicit programmatic associations. Refactoring these fields into standalone containers with an explicit `<label htmlFor={id}>` coupled with `aria-invalid` and `aria-describedby` linked to validation elements (marked with `role="alert"`) ensures a much more robust and compliant screen-reader experience.
**Action:** Use standalone containers with explicit `htmlFor` and `id` linking, along with programmatic error association, rather than relying on automatic nested label mapping.

## 2026-07-25 - Floating Layout Helpers and Responsive Icon Accessibility
**Learning:** Floating layout helpers (such as persistent floating music controllers, chat widgets, and voice guides) provide critical micro-interactions for emotional warmth and navigation. However, responsive design often hides accompanying text on smaller/mobile viewports (e.g. `hidden md:inline`), leaving them as icon-only controls. To remain fully accessible to screen readers, these buttons must carry explicit, dynamic `aria-label`, `aria-pressed`, and `aria-expanded` attributes matching their internal states.
**Action:** Always provide explicit ARIA accessibility states (`aria-label`, `aria-pressed`, `aria-expanded`) on floating/fixed micro-interaction widgets, particularly when their textual labels are responsively styled to hide on smaller screens.
